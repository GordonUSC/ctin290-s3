Second Text, Coco, and Color as a Place
Arcane splits its palette by class and district. Coco splits it by world. The land of the living is warm, dim and domestic; the Land of the Dead is saturated to a degree that would be unbearable if you had not just spent twenty minutes in the muted version. Watch it, then ask the question that actually matters for their own projects. Not which palette is prettier, but what each one is FOR. A palette is a rule you set and then keep, and the emotional payload arrives at the moment you break it. Second question, and hold them to it: name one color choice in Coco that is cultural rather than universal, and say how you know.

Synthesis 1, What a Color Script Is
A row of small paintings, in story order, showing the color and mood of every beat, made before production starts. Not what happens in a scene, how it feels, using only temperature, value and saturation. Ralph Eggleston brought the color script to Pixar in 1992 as art director on Toy Story, working in pastel on black paper because it was fast and cheap. Lasseter could read the emotional arc of the whole film off a wall of them.
You can read the emotional arc of a film off its color alone, sound off, no dialogue.
12:00
·
Synthesis 2, They Build One
Each student makes a color script on paper for a story they know or one they want to make. Six to eight swatches, in order, left to right, one word underneath each naming the emotion. The constraint that makes it work: you have to set a rule and then break it once, and you have to be able to say what the rule was and where it broke.
A mood board is a color script. This is the rehearsal for the 20 percent assignment.

Synthesis 3, Critique
Pin them up or pass them around. Take three or four and run the course framework on each one.
I LIKE, specific praise. I WISH, name a gap without prescribing. WHAT IF, offer a possibility, not a command.

Exit Ticket, Two Lines Before Anyone Leaves
One term you learned today and where you saw it. One thing you are still unsure of. Collect them at the door. This costs five minutes and it is the cheapest instrument you have for finding out what actually landed. The two things students are least likely to volunteer are which concept went past them and what they believe is expected of them, and this asks for both directly. Read them before the next session and open with the most common confusion, by name, so the room learns that saying you are lost actually changes what happens.

Terms for today

Color ScriptA sequence of images or notations showing the intended color palette and mood progression across a scene or film.
LightingThe quality, direction, and color of illumination. Hard vs. soft light; high-key vs. low-key; motivated vs. stylized.
PaletteThe selected range of colors in a shot, scene, or project; establishes mood and visual unity.
Warm/CoolColor temperature: warm colors (red, orange, yellow) feel intimate and energetic; cool colors (blue, green) feel distant and calm.
